Network / FIELD GUIDE

Reachability is a fact
Trust is a separate question

Service discovery, segmentation, protocol configuration, wireless access and the limits of packet-level observations.

01 / FIELD NOTES

Discover from a named vantage

The same asset can appear different from outside, a user VLAN or a management network.

Combine existing inventory and passive observations with bounded active discovery. Record source location, protocol, destination, time, resolver and test identity. Nmap port states are observations from that probe path: filtered is uncertainty, and an open port is a listening service rather than proof of vulnerability. Version fingerprints need vendor and configuration validation.

# On a lab service you started yourself on localhost
nmap -sT -p 8767 --reason 127.0.0.1
# Record the observation; do not infer exploitability from an open port

03 / FIELD NOTES

Validate service configuration and session trust

Inventory who may connect, how peers are authenticated and what the service exposes.

For HTTP, TLS, DNS, SSH, SMB and administrative services, separate reachability, negotiated protection, peer verification and application permission. Check management-plane exposure, legacy options, default accounts and auditability against the platform’s actual configuration. Wireshark explains an observed exchange; packet capture alone cannot reveal encrypted application content without the relevant keys or endpoint instrumentation.

04 / FIELD NOTES

Wireless testing includes identity and physical scope

An RF-visible network is not automatically an authorized target.

Map owned access points, encryption/authentication mode, enterprise certificate validation, guest segmentation and management access. In a lab, compare intended and unintended SSIDs and client trust behavior. Aircrack-ng covers wireless assessment functions; choose a function based on the hypothesis rather than treating disruption or credential recovery as a required stage. Active radio tests need the agreed physical boundary and timing.
