01 / FIELD NOTES

Authentication, authorization and delegation

A valid credential does not explain everything its holder can control.

In AD, principals, groups, object ACLs, computer trust and service identities create a graph of control. Kerberos exchanges tickets through a KDC; NTLM is a separate authentication mechanism, not a type of Kerberos ticket. In modern identity systems, applications, service principals, federation and delegated/application permissions add other paths. Inventory the credential type, lifetime, issuer, audience and permission source.

03 / FIELD NOTES

Measure credential and session controls

Test the agreed identity lifecycle, not just whether login works.

Inspect MFA enrollment and recovery, token/session revocation, privileged-role activation, service-account secret management and certificate-based identity where used. For password auditing, distinguish offline evaluation of approved hash fixtures from online attempts with lockout and business effects. NTLM relay feasibility depends on protocol/channel protections and configuration; do not equate NTLM support with a confirmed relay path.

05 / FIELD NOTES

Human and physical access are testable boundaries

Account recovery, support workflows and visitor access need defined properties too.

For an agreed human-factor exercise, define the identity claim the support process must verify, use test accounts and measure both inappropriate disclosure and successful reporting. A click rate is not a complete security outcome. For physical-access testing, model what an enrolled device, visitor or unattended workstation can reach. Record consent, privacy, physical scope and recovery conditions in the engagement plan.
